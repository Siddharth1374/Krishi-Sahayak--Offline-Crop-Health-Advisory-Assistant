Tomato Early Blight (Alternaria solani)
Symptoms: Dark concentric-ring spots on older/lower leaves first, yellowing around spots, leaf drop.
Organic treatment: Remove and destroy infected leaves. Apply neem oil spray (5ml neem oil per litre
water) every 7 days. Improve air circulation by spacing plants and pruning lower leaves.
Chemical treatment: Mancozeb 75% WP at 2.5g per litre water, spray every 10-14 days, maximum 3
applications per season. Always wear gloves and mask; do not spray within 7 days of harvest.
Prevention: Rotate crops (avoid planting tomato/potato in the same spot for 2 years), use
disease-resistant varieties, avoid overhead watering, mulch to prevent soil splashing onto leaves.

Tomato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)
Symptoms: Water-soaked pale green to brown patches on leaves, white fungal growth on leaf undersides
in humid conditions, rapid spread especially in cool wet weather. Can destroy a crop within days.
Organic treatment: Copper-based fungicides (copper oxychloride, 3g per litre water) applied
preventively before rains. Remove infected plants immediately to stop spread.
Chemical treatment: Metalaxyl + Mancozeb combination product per label dosage; this is an emergency
situation — if more than 25% of the field is affected, consult your local agriculture extension
officer immediately as blight can spread to neighboring farms.
Prevention: Ensure good drainage, avoid dense planting, monitor closely during humid/rainy periods.

Potato Early Blight
Symptoms: Similar target-spot lesions as tomato early blight, on lower leaves first.
Treatment: Same neem oil / mancozeb approach as tomato early blight. Hill soil around the base of
plants to protect tubers from spores washing down.

General fungicide safety notes:
Always read the product label for exact dosage — dosages vary by brand and concentration.
Wear gloves, mask, and eye protection when spraying. Do not spray on windy days. Keep children and
animals away from treated areas for at least 24 hours. Store chemicals away from food and water
sources, out of reach of children.
